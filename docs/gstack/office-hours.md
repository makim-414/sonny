# Design: Hyodo TV, a TV agent that learns from the senior it lives with

Generated by gstack `/office-hours` (startup mode, Phase 2A diagnostic), c0mpiled-17 Seoul, 2026-11-09.
Status: DRAFT (pre-product, hackathon build)

## Problem Statement
An older adult who lives alone spends most of her waking hours in front of the TV. Her daughter visits every third day. Between visits, the daughter knows how her mother is doing only by phoning to check, again and again. Existing senior AI companions (Ato, Kolvia, JoyCalls) reach seniors through phone calls or a small speaker, and they position themselves as care.

## Demand Evidence
- Founder observed the case directly: his grandmother. She lives alone, gets out about once or twice a week (a temple visit, about 4 hours), and otherwise watches TV. She spends a long time each day flipping through about 220 real channels to find one singer (about 40 minutes a day, founder's estimate, not measured).
- Founder ran a gallery for several years whose core clients are retired people aged 65 and over. Founder's read: this group enjoys teaching, has time, and likes feeling useful to society.
- Diagnostic verdict: evidence of pain (observed behavior) is real, but evidence of demand is zero. No senior and no family member has used or paid for anything yet.

## Status Quo
- For the senior: the TV remote. Most remotes already have voice search, so "find a channel by voice" is not a wedge on its own. The diagnostic moved channel control down to one tool among many.
- For the daughter (the buyer): repeated phone calls to check in. Cost: worry between calls, and calls the mother may not pick up.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- Daily user: the senior. Paying user: the adult daughter. Her current workaround is phoning to check.
- Wedge: an agent on the TV that acts as a student. The senior teaches it (a recipe, a story, a grandchild's name). It remembers, speaks first the next day using what it learned, and does small tasks on request. The daughter sees an activity feed of what her mother asked the agent to do, with privacy levels the senior controls. She no longer has to phone just to check.

## Constraints
- 5-hour build. Real deployment (an HDMI stick or passthrough box over the existing TV, real set-top control by IR or HDMI-CEC) is out of scope today. The demo is a web app virtual TV on a real monitor, a phone microphone remote, a QR trial for judges, and a family phone app.
- Korean EPG data has no public API. Schedule data is seeded and labeled as demo data.
- Privacy is a baseline: no camera, and the senior chooses what is shared.

## Premises
1. Seniors would rather be useful than be cared for. Positioning as a learner, not a caregiver, removes the "don't treat me as old" objection and makes the product something to brag about. This is our distribution edge. Unverified.
2. The senior will talk to the TV if the TV speaks first and lowers its own volume while she answers. Unverified.
3. The daughter will pay a monthly fee for an activity feed that replaces check-in calls. Unverified, no price yet.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Voice channel finder ("play the singer I like")
Clear pain, demo-friendly. Rejected as the core because carrier remotes already have voice search, and the founder clarified that channel finding is not the point.
### Approach B: Full assistant (talk, proactive check-ins, memory, schedule, news, family link, TV control)
Matches the long-term vision. Too wide for a wedge: judges remember none of seven half-built features.
### Approach C (recommended): The student agent, plus a family activity feed
One loop, end to end: the senior teaches, the agent remembers and speaks first, it acts with a tool call (a channel change or reminder), and the daughter's phone gets the update.

## Recommended Approach
Approach C. The pitch line: "Every senior companion sells care. Seniors don't want to be cared for, they want to be useful. Ours is a TV agent that learns from them."
Why now: realtime speech-to-speech APIs and persistent agent memory became cheap and usable in late 2026, and there is now real demand for human-taught post-training data. That gives seniors' time and knowledge a use it never had. Revenue stays with the family subscription. Data contribution is framed as social value, not as the business model, to avoid reading as exploitative.

## Open Questions
- Does the grandmother actually enjoy teaching? The founder has not seen a scene of it yet. To be checked with the mother.
- Has she ever used the remote's voice button? If not, why not?
- What would the mother pay per month?

## Success Criteria (today)
- A judge scans the QR code, speaks, and sees the TV act, remember, and notify the family phone, live, with no hand-holding.
- At least one real reaction from a senior or the mother, quoted in the pitch.

## Distribution Plan
First channel: the founder's gallery clients, all retired and over 65, pitched as "teach an AI" rather than "get a companion." Later: bundles with carrier set-tops and TV makers.

## Dependencies
Realtime voice API, LLM tool calling, a small memory store, push notifications to the family app.

## The Assignment
Before 12:00, text three gallery clients two versions of the pitch: "an AI companion for when you're alone" versus "teach an AI from your experience, used for public-good AI training." Count the replies. Phone the mother with three questions: has the grandmother enjoyed teaching someone, has she used the voice remote, and what would the mother pay each month.
