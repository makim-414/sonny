# Pitch (under 2 minutes, English)

Spoken script first, about 295 words (about 1:50 at 160 words per minute; rehearse once with a timer and cut the vision line if it runs over). Then the judging map, one-line answers, sources and likely questions.

## Spoken script

**Hook (0:00)**
Pickle covers your whole workday through Zoom. We cover grandma's whole day through her TV.

**Problem (0:08)**
My grandmother lives alone. My mom sleeps over every third night. Her only outing is the temple, once or twice a week. The rest of the day, the TV is on.
In Korea, more than one in five people is now 65 or older, and 38 percent of senior households are one person, alone.

**Why TV (0:25)**
She will never install an app. But she already sits in front of one big screen all day.

**Solution (0:32)**
So we put a full AI assistant inside her TV. It speaks first: "Did you take your pills? Rain later, take an umbrella." This morning my mom booked her a taxi to the hospital on her phone. Now grandma just says "call me a taxi", the plate number shows up in huge letters, and mom gets a heads-up.
It orders groceries, but anything that costs money waits for mom's one-tap approval. It carries mom's messages to the screen and sends grandma's answer back as a summary. At night, it turns on her favorite show.

**Why we win (1:05)**
Ato and the phone companions sell care, and they talk. Grandma doesn't want care, she wants to be useful. So our agent is her student: she teaches it, "my daughter comes every third day, cook for two", and it remembers, then plans groceries with it on its own.

**Why now and how (1:22)**
Agents can finally call real tools, not just chat. Ours is one Claude agent with memory, scheduling, rides, orders, TV guide and family messages, Korean voice in and out. Built today, in five hours.

**Vision (1:38)**
Today, an app on her TV. Next, the TV itself. Then multiplayer: seniors meeting each other by voice through the TV they already have. Her temple friends, on the couch, every day.

## How this maps to the three judging criteria

| Criterion | What we say | What we show |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity of problem and solution | One real person, one real day. Alone, TV all day, family visits every third night. Our answer: an assistant that lives where she already is. | Demo follows her day from 7:50 am to 8 pm. Every scene ends with something done, not something said. |
| Technical implementation | One agent, many tools, real actions with a human approval gate. Proactive triggers on a fast-forwarding clock. Memory taught by the user. | TV screen and mom's phone update live from the same activity log. Taxi and grocery cards, approve on the phone, the TV reacts. |
| Creativity | Two inversions. The interface is the TV, not a new device. The relationship is student and teacher, not patient and caretaker. | Grandma teaches a fact at 1 pm; the agent uses it unprompted at 3 pm. |

## One-line answers

- **Why TV?** It is the one screen she already watches for hours every day, so there is nothing new to learn, and it is big enough for faces, photos and a taxi plate number she can actually read.
- **Why now?** Korea crossed into a super-aged society in December 2024, and agents can now call real tools (book a ride, place an order) instead of just chatting, which is what turns a companion into an assistant.
- **How are we different from Ato?** Ato is a screen-free speaker that talks and relays messages; we live on the screen she already watches, we act (rides, groceries, shows) with the family approving anything that costs money, and she is the teacher, not the patient.
- **Who pays?** The adult child, as a monthly family subscription (estimate, not yet validated). Later, bundled with the set-top box by Korean pay-TV operators.
- **Privacy?** No camera. The family sees what the agent did (requests, messages, medicine check-ins). What grandma teaches stays hers unless she shares it.

## Likely questions

- **"Isn't an agent ordering things for an 80-year-old dangerous?"** Anything that spends money or leaves the house goes to the family first. In the demo, the grocery order waits for mom's tap. The taxi sends mom a heads-up.
- **"Will she really talk to a TV?"** The TV talks first, by name, at the times she already watches. That is the habit loop. We still need to test this with her; it is our riskiest assumption.
- **"Why not just a smart speaker?"** She cannot read a plate number off a speaker, and she does not sit in front of one all day.
- **"How does it control a real TV?"** Today: a TV web app (Android TV sideload or the TV browser). Next: our own box or a pay-TV operator's set-top box.
- **"What is multiplayer AI here?"** Seniors meeting each other by voice on their TVs, with the agent as host: introducing, reminding, keeping the conversation going. No betting or gambling.
